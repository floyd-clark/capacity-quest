# Twelve interview response drills

Use 30s for signal/risk/action/outcome; use 90s to add alternatives, authority, evidence and a truthful firsthand example. These are outlines, not invented biographies or predictions of interview questions.

## 1. How do you diagnose a multi-rack fault across several domains?
W6 / Ch.15

Outline: Set TRIAGE roles; bound impact; time-align raw telemetry; test highest-information hypothesis; communicate on a clock.

## 2. How do logs, firmware matrices, counters and configuration lineage isolate failures?
W1,W2,W4 / Ch.5,7

Outline: Pin versions and source timestamps; compare controlled cohorts; change one factor; preserve raw evidence and reproducibility.

## 3. What gates distinguish installed hardware from serviceable capacity?
W5 / Ch.3,7,16

Outline: Physical → Functional → Integrated → Operational; every mandatory dependency plus acceptance authority and workload evidence.

## 4. What do you do with a missed DVT gate before a delivery deadline?
W2 / Ch.7,8

Outline: State missing coverage and risk; offer hold or bounded qualified scope; name authority and closure evidence; never relabel boot as DVT.

## 5. How do you prioritize when every team says its domain is healthy?
W5,W6 / Ch.5,15

Outline: Find the mandatory cross-domain dependency; use fresh integrated evidence rather than averaged colors; assign one accountable test lead.

## 6. What if supplier data conflicts with internal validation?
W1,W6 / Ch.10

Outline: Reconcile serials, denominator, exposure and test revision; contain disputed material; agree independent retest and prevention evidence.

## 7. How do you choose rollback, limited rollout or hold?
W2,W6 / Ch.6,16

Outline: Compare qualified baseline, impact, reversibility and gate status; canary only approved scope; record stop criteria and decision authority.

## 8. How do you organize owners without replacing technical experts?
W4,W6 / Ch.11–14

Outline: Technical leads own investigation; program/incident lead owns dependencies, decisions and rhythm; record owner, due time and test.

## 9. What belongs in a credible 15-minute incident update?
W6 / Ch.15

Outline: Fact → consequence → options/decision → owner → time/evidence; separate unknowns; report containment and next checkpoint.

## 10. What evidence permits a deployment wave to restart?
W5,W6 / Ch.7,16

Outline: Current pinned configuration; representative integrated and recovery tests; canary scope and rollback; signed authority.

## 11. How do you prevent a workaround becoming fleet debt?
W6 / Ch.10,16

Outline: Track temporary scope/expiry and root cause separately; verify correction; regression-test prevention; control configuration propagation.

## 12. How do you explain ambiguity to a director without overclaiming?
W6 / Ch.11–14,17

Outline: Bound facts and confidence; quantify impact only where evidenced; offer options; make the decision and owner explicit; specify next evidence time.
